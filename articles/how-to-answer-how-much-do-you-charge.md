# How to Answer "How Much Do You Charge?" (Cleaning Business Reply Template)
> A copy-paste reply that turns price shoppers into booked cleanings, plus a simple way to quote by labor hours.

"How much do you charge?" is the message every cleaning business gets the most, and the one most owners answer badly. Send a flat number and you either scare people off or underprice the job. Send a long explanation and they move on to the next cleaner who replied faster.

The fix is a reply that answers right away, sounds professional, and gets you the details you need to quote properly.

## The reply template

Copy this and save it as a quick reply in Messenger or your phone's text shortcuts:

> Thanks for reaching out! Prices depend on the size of the home and what you'd like included. Could you share the number of bedrooms and bathrooms, how often you'd like cleaning, and anything extra (oven, fridge, windows)? I'll send you a clear quote today.

## Why it works

- **It answers in seconds.** Speed wins most cleaning inquiries. People message several businesses at once.
- **It doesn't throw out a number blind.** A 1-bedroom apartment and a 4-bedroom house with pets are different jobs.
- **It asks for exactly what you need.** Bedrooms, bathrooms, frequency and extras cover most of the time difference between jobs.
- **It promises a next step.** "I'll send you a clear quote today" keeps the conversation yours.

## How to price once they answer

Quote from **labor hours**, not time on site. Two cleaners for two hours is four labor hours, not two.

1. Estimate the labor hours for the home (your own past jobs are the best guide).
2. Multiply by your hourly rate.
3. Add supplies, travel and each extra (oven, fridge, inside windows) as their own lines.
4. Round to a clean number and send it with what's included.

Separate line items make the price easier for the client to accept, because they can see what they're paying for, and easier for you to adjust when they ask for "just the basics".

## Follow up once

If they go quiet for a day, send one short follow-up: "Hi! Just checking you got the quote. Happy to adjust anything or answer questions." One follow-up books a surprising number of jobs. More than that starts to feel pushy.
