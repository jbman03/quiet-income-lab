# How to Keep Your Cleaning Clients (and Raise Prices Without Losing Them)
> Why clients leave, the simple touchpoints that keep them, and how to announce a price increase kindly.

Finding a new cleaning client takes ads, posts, quotes and follow-ups. Keeping one takes a few well-timed messages and consistent work. Industry surveys rank customer retention among cleaning businesses' biggest challenges, so it's worth getting right.

## Why clients actually leave

Clients rarely leave over a few dollars. The usual reasons are:

- **Inconsistent quality:** something gets missed, or each visit feels different.
- **Slow or no replies:** a question or complaint sits unanswered.
- **A different cleaner every time:** trust is personal in someone's home.
- **Feeling taken for granted:** no contact except the invoice.

All four are fixable.

## Use the same checklist every visit

Consistency is the number one retention tool. A written scope checklist means nothing gets skipped, a new team member cleans the same way, and you can show a client exactly what's included if there's ever a question.

## Message at the right moments

A recurring client should hear from you at a few predictable moments:

1. The day before every visit (a reminder) and an "on my way" text.
2. Right after the first clean, then a review request the next day.
3. After the second or third visit: "Anything you'd like us to focus on?"
4. At three months: a thank-you and a referral ask.
5. At six months: offer a seasonal deep clean.
6. At one year: a thank-you, and your yearly price review if needed.

## Handle complaints the same day

When something goes wrong, reply the same day, say sorry without excuses, and offer to come back and fix it. A complaint handled well often makes a client more loyal than before.

## Raising prices without losing clients

Costs go up every year, and your prices should too. The way you announce it matters more than the amount:

- **Give notice.** At least 30 days is a kind default.
- **Thank them first.** Lead with appreciation, not the number.
- **Give one honest reason.** Rising supply and fuel costs, or paying your team fairly.
- **Say what stays the same.** Same schedule, same cleaner, same care.
- **Keep the increase modest** and review prices once a year instead of surprising people.
