3 Scams Hitting Small Cleaning Businesses (and the 10-Minute Fixes)

Stolen Facebook pages, fake invoices and hijacked payment accounts, and the simple settings that stop them.

Small service businesses are prime targets because the owner is busy, works from a phone, and runs everything through a handful of accounts. These are the three scams that hit cleaning businesses most, and how to shut each one down.

1. "Your page will be disabled"

A message arrives in Messenger or email saying your Facebook page broke a rule or a copyright claim and will be shut down unless you "verify" through a link. The link is a fake login page. Once they have your login, they take the page, remove you as admin, and sometimes run ads on your card.

The fix (10 minutes):

  • Turn on two-factor authentication on the personal Facebook account that owns the page.
  • Add a second trusted admin (a spouse or partner) so the page can be recovered.
  • Remember that Meta never asks you to log in through a link in a message. Go to facebook.com yourself instead.

2. Fake invoices and "new bank details"

You get an invoice from a "supplier" or an email saying a vendor changed banks. Pay it and the money's gone. A variation targets you as the business: a fake "client" overpays by check and asks you to send the difference back.

The fix:

  • Verify any change in bank details by calling a number you already have, not one in the message.
  • Never refund an overpayment until the original payment has fully cleared, and even then be suspicious.

3. Hijacked email, then everything else

Your business email is the master key. Whoever controls it can reset your booking software, payment account and Facebook. Attackers who get in often add a quiet forwarding rule so they see every password-reset email.

The fix:

  • Turn on two-step verification for your business email (Gmail: Google Account, Security, 2-Step Verification).
  • Check for forwarding rules you didn't create (Gmail: Settings, Forwarding).
  • Turn on login and payout alerts in Square, Stripe, PayPal and your bank.

Two more quick wins

  • Client door and alarm codes: don't keep them in plain notes on a shared phone or in group chats.
  • Former employees: remove their access to your booking app, shared email and Google Drive the day they leave.
