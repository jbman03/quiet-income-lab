How to Start a House Cleaning Business: A Practical Step-by-Step Guide

The real steps, in order: costs, paperwork, pricing, first clients, and keeping them, with a free startup cost calculator.

Starting a house cleaning business is one of the lowest-cost businesses you can start. You can begin with a vacuum, a supply caddy and a phone. The hard parts aren't the cleaning. They're pricing it right, finding the first clients, and keeping them.

Here's the order to do things in.

1. Add up your real costs first

Before you set prices, know what it costs to start and to run each month: registration, insurance, a good vacuum, supplies, fuel and your phone. Use the free startup cost calculator and replace each placeholder with local prices. It also tells you how many cleanings a month cover your costs.

2. Make it official

  • Pick a name and check that it isn't already registered in your state and that the Facebook page name is free.
  • Register the business with your state. Many owners start as a sole proprietor and form an LLC once they grow. Your state's Secretary of State website explains the options and fees.
  • Get general liability insurance before your first job. Clients ask, and one broken item can cost more than a year of premiums. Some owners also get bonded.
  • Open a separate bank account so business money never mixes with personal money. It makes taxes far easier.

Rules differ by state and city, so check your local requirements for business licenses and sales tax on services.

3. Set your prices

Don't copy the cheapest competitor. Clients who chose you only for price leave the moment someone cheaper shows up. Price from labor hours instead:

  1. Estimate the labor hours for the home (bedrooms, bathrooms, condition, type of clean).
  2. Multiply by your hourly rate.
  3. Add supplies, travel and extras as separate lines.

The free quote calculator does this math and writes the quote message for you.

4. Get your first clients

  • Tell everyone you know. Your first 3-5 clients usually come from friends, family and neighbors.
  • Post in local community groups with a short intro and a before/after photo from a friend's home (with permission).
  • Answer every inquiry fast. People message several cleaners at once, and the first clear reply often wins.
  • Ask every happy client for a review right after the first clean, while they're excited.

5. Keep the clients you win

Keeping a client is far cheaper than finding a new one. Industry surveys rank customer retention among cleaning businesses' top challenges, and slow replies or inconsistent cleans are common reasons clients leave.

  • Offer a small discount for recurring weekly or biweekly cleans.
  • Send a reminder the day before each visit.
  • Use the same checklist every time so the quality is consistent.
  • When something goes wrong, reply the same day and make it right.

6. Save time on messages

Answering messages eats more time than most new owners expect. Save quick replies for the questions you get every week: pricing, availability, what's included, and how to prepare for a visit.
